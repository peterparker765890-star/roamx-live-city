05 / FAMILY HEALTH PROTECTION

Simple ways to protect your family

Affordable steps that can help reduce exposure, especially in homes with children and older people.

🏠 At home

Avoid burning plastic, rubber and rubbish. Keep indoor smoke away from living and sleeping areas. If cooking with solid fuel, improve ventilation and keep children away from the smoke as much as possible.

🍃 Cleaner cooking

Where affordable and available, consider cleaner cooking options such as LPG, electricity or biogas. Do not assume every household can immediately afford a new stove or fuel.

🧹 Reduce dust

Use damp cloths or wet cleaning rather than dry sweeping where practical. Cover stored sand and construction materials. Avoid creating dust near homes and schools.

👨‍👩‍👧 Protect vulnerable people

Children, older adults, pregnant women and people with heart or lung conditions may need extra care. Reduce strenuous outdoor activity during heavy smoke or poor air conditions.

Health warning: seek medical help for severe breathlessness, chest pain, fainting or worsening breathing problems. This website does not diagnose illness or replace a healthcare professional.
06 / VILLAGE-LEVEL IMPACT

Small local actions. Healthier communities.

Pollution prevention becomes stronger when households, schools, farmers and local authorities work together.

Organise waste properly

Separate wet and dry waste, use designated collection points and discourage open burning. Ask the local panchayat about available waste collection services.

Support smoke-free cooking spaces

Share information about cleaner cooking access, safer kitchen ventilation and keeping children away from cooking smoke.

Reduce road and construction dust

Encourage responsible covering of dusty materials, careful handling of construction waste and suitable dust-control measures.

Work with farmers

Promote locally suitable alternatives to crop-residue burning, such as collection, composting, mulching or approved residue-management programmes.

Involve schools and local bodies

Hold awareness sessions, identify recurring smoke sources and share concerns with the panchayat or relevant pollution-control authorities.
